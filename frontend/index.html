Methodology

ThermoScope processes NASA FIRMS satellite fire/thermal detections through a fixed, documented pipeline. This page describes exactly what the system does and does not do.

FIRMS→ Events→ Evidence→ Silver labels→ LightGBM→ NRT inference

1. FIRMS detections

Detections come from NASA's FIRMS Area API for the VIIRS S-NPP instrument, restricted to a fixed Gujarat bounding box (20.0–24.7°N, 68.0–74.5°E). Two sources are used: VIIRS_SNPP_SP (standard-processing, science-quality, used for the historical record and the earlier part of 2026) and VIIRS_SNPP_NRT (near-real-time, used for the ongoing 2026 update window). Each detection is a single satellite observation with a location, time, fire radiative power (FRP), and day/night flag.

This fixed rectangular bounding box is a working-assumption study area, not a precise political boundary — near its corners it can include small slivers of neighboring territory (for example, part of the Rann of Kutch border region in the northwest). A handful of real, correctly-plotted events near that corner may therefore appear close to or across the Gujarat–Pakistan border on the map; their coordinates are shown exactly as measured, never adjusted.

2. Event construction

Individual detections are grouped into events using a spatiotemporal connected-components method: two detections belong to the same event if they are within 750 meters and 1 day of each other, applied transitively across a whole chain of detections. Events with fewer than 2 member detections are discarded as noise. An event's status is closed once at least 1 day has passed since its last detection with no further linkage possible, and provisional otherwise — a provisional event could still be extended by a future detection.

3. Evidence sources

Each event is joined against independent external sources — none of these are model predictions:

  • OpenStreetMap industrial/power — tagged industrial land use, works, and power infrastructure.
  • OpenStreetMap flare points.
  • OpenStreetMap kiln/brickyard points — coverage in Gujarat is extremely sparse in the source data.
  • WRI Global Power Plant Database — coal/gas/oil thermal plants.
  • ESA WorldCover 10m land cover classification at the event centroid.
  • Persistent cluster overlap — whether the event overlaps one of 60 recurring hotspot clusters identified from 2023 detections, and that cluster's recurrence strength.

4. Silver labels

The classifier is trained on silver labels: labels produced by deterministic rules over FRP, night fraction, evidence proximity, and seasonal timing — not human-verified ground truth. Brick Kiln has zero real labeled examples in the underlying data and is not a trained class. Events with insufficient or conflicting rule matches are labeled Unknown/Ambiguous and excluded from training.

5. LightGBM classifier

A single, fixed-configuration LightGBM model is trained once on 2019–2022 silver-labeled historical events, using 11 features (location, spatial footprint, event status, detection-confidence fractions, sensor scan/track geometry, point-cloud elongation, time-of-day consistency, and detections-per-day). The model is never retrained — 2026 data is inference-only.

6. Temporal vs. spatial evaluation

Two different holdout designs were used to evaluate the model. A temporal split (train on 2019–2022, test on 2023–2025) measures whether the model generalizes across time at already-seen locations. A spatial split (holding out entire 0.5°×0.5° geographic grid cells from training) measures whether it generalizes to genuinely new locations.

7. Limitations

  • The model is a forced 4-class classifier — it always outputs one of Crop Residue, Forest/Wildfire, Gas Flare, or Industrial, and cannot output Brick Kiln or "unknown."
  • Industrial and Gas Flare predictions primarily reflect known-site recognition, not general new-source detection.
  • Silver labels are rule-based heuristics, not human-verified ground truth.
  • 2026 data is inference-only and is never used to train or update the model.
  • The Gujarat study area is a fixed rectangular bounding box, not a precise political boundary — see note in Section 1.
